Heyrafiki applies established engineering principles to the risks of Care, Claims and Payments. A principle guides a decision. It does not replace evidence.

## Prefer the smallest complete system

- Keep each fact in one authoritative store.
- Reuse an existing component, service or contract before creating another.
- Remove redundant work before adding capacity.
- Measure the bottleneck before introducing infrastructure.
- Keep provider details behind typed service boundaries.

This is the efficiency lesson from large systems such as Uber: scale comes from reducing repeated work and clarifying ownership, not only from adding machines.

## Build for operability

The Joel Test establishes everyday engineering hygiene. The Twelve-Factor App keeps configuration explicit, dependencies declared and backing services replaceable. Heyrafiki extends those foundations with:

- reviewed migrations and restoration tests;
- audit evidence for sensitive changes;
- idempotent critical operations;
- observable queues, retries and reconciliation;
- runbooks for degraded providers and interrupted connectivity;
- security and Privacy review before release.

## Keep boundaries legible

DRY removes duplicated knowledge, not every repeated line. Separation of Concerns keeps product surfaces independent from provider SDKs. Composition Over Inheritance keeps behavior replaceable. The Principle of Least Surprise keeps names, states and outcomes predictable.

KISS and SOLID matter when they reduce cognitive load. Do not add an abstraction until it has a clear owner, a real consumer and a failure mode the Team can operate.

## Improve the code you touch

The Boy Scout Rule is scoped. Leave the area you changed clearer than you found it, while preserving unrelated working behavior. A focused improvement with verification is safer than a broad cleanup with unclear outcomes.
